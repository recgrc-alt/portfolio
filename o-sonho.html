O Sonho (Zobeide)

2025 · Motion & Audiovisual · Co-Director · Editor · Sound Designer

With Ana Oliveira · Rafael Campos

A short film built out of Zobeide, one of Italo Calvino's invisible cities. Five strangers dream the same woman on the same foggy street, and spend their lives chasing her. No dialogue, six minutes, shot at night in Coimbra.

I picked a city that does not exist

Zobeide, out of Calvino

The brief started from Italo Calvino's Invisible Cities, and each of us chose one to work from. I took Zobeide, and the reasons were written down before anything was shot.

The first was the subject. Zobeide is about desire and obsession, about chasing something that is never caught, which is a thing everybody recognises whether or not they have read the book.

The second was colder and just as important. Zobeide is a city of repeating streets and closed spaces, which means it can be built with almost nothing. A student film that needs a spectacle is a student film that fails, and this one could be carried by symbol and mood instead.

Five men and the same dream

A mason, a navigator, an architect, a visionary, a cartographer

Five men who have never met dream the same thing on the same night: narrow streets under fog, and the silhouette of a woman who turns a corner and is gone before they reach her.

They wake up with the dream still sharp, and each one goes looking for it. Their paths cross, and together they set about making the place from the dream real. Time passes, what they have built grows, and so does the doubt about whether any of it was ever worth it.

Then a woman walks into it, carrying a suitcase, and nobody knows who she is. That is the end, and it is where the film finally lets the tension go.

Each man has a trade and a room of his own, and the film introduces them the same way: a corridor, a bed, a small gesture. Taking off shoes. Taking off glasses. Blowing out a candle.

Not one word is spoken

The sound was written before the picture

There is no dialogue anywhere in the film, and that was decided at the start rather than arrived at by accident. The story is about something nobody can explain, including to themselves, so letting the characters explain it would have undone it.

What replaces dialogue is a sound list that existed before we shot a frame: footsteps that repeat until they turn into a loop, a clock, blankets, the calm breathing of someone asleep and the ragged breathing of someone waking badly, drunk voices somewhere out in the street, wind.

The sound was the part I took on most of, and in a film with nothing being said it is not accompaniment, it is where half the meaning lives.

Coimbra at night, lit by a car

Borrowed clothes, real fog, four nights

It was shot in Coimbra, in the streets of the city, and in the houses of people we know, because a film with five men who each need a bedroom needs five bedrooms and no budget for a set.

The costume is period, late nineteenth into the twentieth century, and it came from the local folk dance group, who lent it to us. The actors are colleagues of ours.

The light was a car. We used the headlights, pointed where the shot needed them, which is the sort of solution that is only embarrassing until you see it on screen. And the fog, which the whole dream depends on, is not a machine and is not an effect: it is the actual night mist that Coimbra has anyway, and we shot on the nights it was there.

Four nights of filming and something close to five hundred takes, in 4K, for six minutes of finished film.

The labyrinth is in the edit

We never built a city

The story has them raising a city out of their dream. We did not build one, and we never intended to. That is the gap every adaptation has to solve and it is the part of the project I am most pleased with.

The labyrinth is made in the cutting room. Actors walking and running through real streets, shot from angles that hide where they are, cut so that they seem to pass the same corner more than once and to arrive where they started. Close shots for how alone each man is, wide ones for how big the thing is that they are inside of.

The audience is given the feeling of a labyrinth instead of the sight of one, and a feeling is cheaper and lasts longer.

The one that went best

And the thanks it is owed

Of everything I made at university, this is the project that was received best. I am not going to put the mark on a website, but it was the top of anything I have done.

It is also the most conceptual thing here. There is no dialogue, no explanation and nothing in it that tells you what to feel, and it asks the person watching to meet it somewhere. That was the risk of it and it is the reason it worked.

It was made with Ana Oliveira and Rafael Campos, and it does not exist without the five actors who gave it their nights. The delivery also included a moving poster and a set of teasers, cut from the same material.
